# Data Processing Agreement

**Version 2026-11.** Between **{company}** (the "Customer") and **Sharet Africa**, operator of scharge (the "Processor").

> Draft prepared for review. Have it checked by a Kenyan advocate before relying on it.

## 1. Who owns the data

1.1 All data the Customer, its staff, tenants, owners and guests put into scharge, or that scharge creates for the Customer (properties, units, tenants, leases, bills, payments, meter readings, messages, documents and the Customer's website content), is the **Customer's data**.

1.2 Under the Data Protection Act, 2019 (Kenya), the Customer is the **data controller** and the Processor is a **data processor** acting only on the Customer's behalf.

1.3 The Processor does not sell, rent or share the Customer's data. It does not use the data for its own purposes, except as allowed in clause 1.4.

1.4 The one exception: the Processor may use counts that are combined across all customers and anonymised (for example, the total number of units on scharge). These never identify the Customer or any person.

## 2. What the Processor does with the data

2.1 The Processor processes the Customer's data only to provide scharge as described on its website and in the dashboard. That covers:
- hosting the dashboard, the tenant portal and the Customer's website;
- billing, collecting payments and sending reminders;
- KYC checks;
- support;
- keeping backups.

2.2 The Processor follows the Customer's documented instructions. The settings the Customer chooses in the dashboard (for example, switching rent reminders or document reading on) are such instructions.

## 3. Confidentiality and security

3.1 Only Processor staff who need access to provide the service or support may access the Customer's data. They are bound by confidentiality.

3.2 The Processor protects the data with appropriate technical and organisational measures, including:
- encrypted connections (HTTPS);
- separation of each customer's records;
- role-based access;
- encrypted storage of credentials;
- private storage of identity documents;
- daily backups.

## 4. Sub-processors

4.1 The Processor uses these sub-processors:
- the hosting provider of its servers;
- Safaricom (M-Pesa);
- Sharet Africa payment services;
- KenyaSMS (SMS);
- Meta (WhatsApp), only when the Customer turns WhatsApp on;
- Anthropic (reading uploaded KYC documents), only while document reading is on;
- the email provider the Customer or the Processor configures.

4.2 The Processor gives the Customer 30 days' notice before adding a sub-processor that receives personal data. The Customer may object during that period.

## 5. People's rights

The Processor helps the Customer answer requests from tenants and others to see, correct, delete or move their personal data. The dashboard and the full data export support this.

## 6. Breaches

6.1 The Processor tells the Customer without undue delay, and within 48 hours of becoming aware of it, of any breach affecting the Customer's data.

6.2 The notice will say:
- what happened;
- what data is affected;
- what has been done about it.

6.3 This lets the Customer notify the Data Commissioner within 72 hours, as the Act requires.

## 7. Transfers outside Kenya

Where a sub-processor processes data outside Kenya, the Processor relies on the safeguards required by Part VI of the Act and the Data Protection (General) Regulations.

## 8. The Customer's copy, backups and leaving

8.1 The Customer may download a full export of its data at any time (Settings › White label & data). It may also receive a nightly copy on storage it controls.

8.2 When the agreement ends, the Customer has 30 days to export its data. The Processor then deletes it from the live system. Copies in backups are deleted as those backups expire, within 90 days.

## 9. Audits

The Processor makes available the information needed to show compliance with this agreement. It allows reasonable audits on 30 days' written notice, at the Customer's cost, no more than once a year.

## 10. General

10.1 This agreement forms part of the scharge terms of service. Where the two differ on personal data, this agreement wins.

10.2 Kenyan law governs this agreement, and the courts of Kenya have jurisdiction.

Accepted in the scharge dashboard by the Customer's authorised signatory. The name, role, date and IP address are recorded.
